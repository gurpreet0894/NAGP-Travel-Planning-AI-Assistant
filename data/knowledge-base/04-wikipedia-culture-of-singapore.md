# Culture of Singapore (Wikipedia)

## Ethnic Groups & Demographics

Singapore's population exceeds 5.47 million, comprising Chinese, Malays, Indians, Eurasians
and mixed communities including Peranakans (descendants of Chinese immigrants with Malay or
Indonesian heritage).

## Key Cultural Principles

- **Meritocracy** — the education system emphasises that the best and brightest, regardless
  of race, religion and socio-economic background, should develop their potential.
- **Racial Harmony** — celebrated through initiatives like Racial Harmony Day (21 July). The
  Housing Development Board enforces an Ethnic Integration Policy to maintain multicultural
  balance in public housing.

## Major Religions

Buddhism, Christianity, Islam, Taoism and Hinduism are the primary faiths, with government
emphasis on respecting all beliefs.

## Ethnic Neighbourhoods (good for cultural experiences)

- **Katong** — Peranakan heritage area with colourful shophouses (conservation area since
  1993).
- **Kampong Glam & Geylang Serai** — Malay cultural focal points; Hari Raya Bazaars during
  Ramadan.
- **Little India** — South Indian vegetarian food and vibrant street life.
- **Chinatown** — historic Chinese cultural quarter.

## Major Festivals & Public Holidays

Chinese New Year, Vesak Day (Buddhist), Hari Raya Puasa (Eid ul-Fitr), Deepavali (Hindu
Diwali), Christmas & Good Friday, National Day (9 August).

## Cuisine Culture

Singapore's hawker centres exemplify culinary fusion — Malay stalls sell Chinese and Tamil
dishes, and Chinese vendors incorporate Malay ingredients and techniques. Popular dishes
include chilli crab. Hawker culture gained UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage status in
2020.

## Languages

Four main languages: **English** (first language in schools), **Mandarin Chinese**
(official language for the ethnic Chinese majority), **Malay** (the national language,
used in the anthem and military commands), and **Tamil** (official language for South Asian
communities). Most Singaporeans are bilingual, speaking English plus a mother-tongue
language.

## Arts & Performing Arts

**Esplanade – Theatres on the Bay** (2003) is the major performing-arts venue, nicknamed
"The Durian" for its architecture. Singapore has an active comedy scene with weekly shows.

## Parks & Gardens as Cultural Priorities

The **Singapore Botanic Gardens** holds UNESCO World Heritage status — the only tropical
garden with this honour. **Gardens by the Bay** features the iconic Supertree Grove. This
emphasis on greenery dates back to Lee Kuan Yew's 1963 vision for a "Garden City."

## Practical Cultural Notes for Visitors

- Public art/murals require government permission; unauthorised graffiti is illegal under
  the Vandalism Act.
- Most ethnic and cultural neighbourhoods are easily accessible via the MRT.
- Singapore is often described as where "East meets West," a "Gateway to Asia," and a
  "Garden City."
