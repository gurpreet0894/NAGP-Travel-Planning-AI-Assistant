# Singapore Travel Guide (Wikivoyage)

## Overview

Singapore is a city-state in Southeast Asia founded as a British trading colony in 1819.
Modern Singapore is one of the world's most prosperous countries and has one of the world's
busiest ports. The nation combines contemporary urban development with multicultural
traditions, earning nicknames like the "Lion City" (from Sanskrit origins) and the
"Garden City" (with over 50% green coverage).

## Districts & Neighbourhoods

### Central Business District (CBD) area
- **Riverside / Civic District** — Colonial heritage with museums, restaurants and bars
  centred at Boat Quay and Clarke Quay.
- **Orchard Road** — Major shopping district with extensive malls.
- **Marina Bay** — Modern skyline featuring Marina Bay Sands, Gardens by the Bay and the
  Singapore Flyer.
- **Bugis & Kampong Glam** — Historic Malay district with vibrant nightlife.
- **Chinatown** — Heritage area with restored shophouses.
- **Little India** — South Asian cultural neighbourhood.

### Outer Singapore
- **Sentosa & Harbourfront** — Resort island with Universal Studios Singapore and the
  Southern Ridges treetop walk.
- **East Coast** — Residential areas, Changi Airport, beaches and Pulau Ubin.
- **North & West** — Industrial/residential zones, Mandai Zoo complex.
- **Balestier, Newton, Novena, Toa Payoh** — Budget accommodation and local housing estates.

## Understand: Culture & Context

Singapore's roughly 5.8 million residents comprise Chinese (~75%), Malays (~14%), Indians
(~9%), plus Eurasians, Peranakans and other communities. At least a third of the population
has arrived from elsewhere, including Filipinos, Japanese and Thais.

Singapore is one of the most religiously diverse countries in the world, with Buddhism
(largest, ~33%), Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Taoism, and officially recognised Sikhism,
Zoroastrianism, Judaism, Baha'i and Jainism. About 17% identify as irreligious.

### Climate
Tropical equatorial climate. Average temperatures are around 31°C (88°F) daytime and 24.5°C
(76°F) at night from December–January, and around 32.5°C (90.5°F) daytime / 25.5°C (77.9°F)
at night the rest of the year. Rain falls almost daily; the northeast monsoon
(November–January) brings heavier precipitation. Haze from Sumatran forest fires can occur
May–October.

### Language
English is the primary language for roughly half the population and is widely spoken.
"Singlish" (a local English patois) incorporates Chinese, Malay and Tamil elements. Official
languages are Mandarin Chinese, Malay, Tamil and English.

## Get In

- Passport valid for a minimum of 6 months is required.
- Citizens of Australia, the EU, New Zealand, Norway, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, the UK
  and the USA get visa-free entry for 90 days; most other nationalities get 30-day visa-free
  entry.
- Complete the SG Arrival Card online (at least 3 days before arrival).
- **Changi Airport (SIN)** is the main hub — well organised, four terminals, Jewel Changi
  shopping mall with the Rain Vortex. Ground transport: Grab/Gojek rideshare, taxis, or the
  MRT (~45 minutes to the city, ~S$2.30 adult fare). Trains run roughly 5:30am–11:15pm
  (6am on Sundays/holidays).
- Land/sea options exist from Malaysia (Woodlands Causeway, Tuas Second Link, KTMB shuttle
  train) and from Indonesia/Malaysia via ferry terminals at HarbourFront and Tanah Merah.

## Get Around

- **MRT (Mass Rapid Transit)** — modern, efficient train system across the island. Use an
  EZ-Link card or a contactless credit/debit card.
- **Buses** — extensive network; EZ-Link cards accepted; early morning to late night service.
- **Taxis** — readily available; metered fares or app-based booking via Grab.
- **Rideshare** — Grab and Gojek operate throughout Singapore.
- **Car** — left-side driving; car rental is available but rarely necessary given the quality
  of public transport.
- **Bicycle** — a growing network of cycling paths with bike rental services.
- **On foot** — the compact city centre is very walkable with excellent pedestrian
  infrastructure.

## See: Attractions

- **Gardens by the Bay** — futuristic gardens with Supertrees and climate-controlled domes.
- **Marina Bay Sands** — iconic integrated resort with casino, museum and shopping.
- **Singapore Flyer** — observation wheel with panoramic city views.
- **Sentosa Island** — resort island with Universal Studios Singapore, beaches and
  entertainment.
- **Museums** — Singapore History Museum, Asian Civilisations Museum, Indian Heritage Centre.
- **Religious sites** — Sri Mariamman Temple (Chinatown), Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple
  (Little India), Thian Hock Keng Temple.
- **Singapore Zoo and the Mandai complex** — four major zoos/wildlife parks in the north.
- **Southern Ridges Walk** — urban treetop walk with local wildlife (outdoor).
- **Pulau Ubin** — a rustic island with nature trails (outdoor).

## Do: Activities

- **Cultural activities** — multi-ethnic neighbourhoods offer temples, museums and heritage
  walks (indoor/outdoor mix — good in any weather since many sites are indoor).
- **Gambling** — Marina Bay Sands and Resorts World Sentosa casinos (indoor).
- **Golf** — multiple championship golf courses (outdoor).
- **Spas & wellness** — a wide range of options from budget to luxury (indoor).
- **Swimming & water sports** — East Coast and Sentosa beaches (outdoor).
- **Snow sports** — SnowCity indoor snow centre (indoor, all-weather).

## Eat: Food & Dining

Singapore's cuisine reflects its multicultural heritage:
- **Peranakan / Nonya** — fusion of Chinese and Malay traditions.
- **Malay** — satay, nasi lemak, rendang.
- **Chinese** — diverse regional styles (Hokkien, Cantonese, Teochew).
- **Indian** — South Indian dosa, Tamil curries, Indian breads.

**Hawker centres** offer affordable food from across Asia; iconic centres include Maxwell
Food Centre and Lau Pa Sat. Dishes typically cost S$3–8. Vegetarian, halal-certified and
Indian-vegetarian options are widely available.

## Drink

Duty-free allowance is 1 litre each of wine, beer and spirits. Alcohol is prohibited for
under-18s. Cigarettes, e-cigarettes and vaping products **cannot** be brought into Singapore.

## Sleep: Accommodation

- **Budget** — hostels and budget hotels; guesthouses in Balestier, Newton, Novena, Toa Payoh.
- **Mid-range** — numerous 3–4 star hotels throughout the city.
- **Luxury** — Marina Bay Sands, Sentosa resorts, high-end Orchard Road properties.

## Stay Safe

- Singapore has very low crime rates; pickpocketing can occur in crowded areas.
- Singapore has very strict drug laws; drug trafficking carries a mandatory death penalty.
- Importing firearms/explosives without a permit is a capital offence.
- Sensitive topics to avoid: criticising the government, comparing/insulting religions,
  and LGBTQ+ issues (decriminalised but the society remains socially conservative).
- Emergency numbers: Fire/Medical **995**, Police **999**.

## Respect: Cultural Considerations

- Remove shoes when entering homes; bring small gifts; accept offered refreshments.
- Business etiquette is formal — business cards are exchanged with both hands.
- Dress modestly and remove shoes/hats when visiting temples and mosques.
- Spitting, littering and importing chewing gum carry fines; loud behaviour is discouraged
  on public transport.

## Cope: Practical Information

- **Electricity** — 230V / 50Hz, BS 1363 (UK-style) plugs.
- **Money** — currency is the Singapore Dollar (SGD); GST is 9%; ATMs and card payments are
  widely available; tipping is not customary.
- **Connectivity** — excellent broadband/mobile coverage; free Wi-Fi in malls and cafés;
  country code +65.

## Holidays & Festivals

Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb), Chingay Parade, Dragon Boat Festival, Hungry Ghost Festival
(~Aug), Mid-Autumn Festival (Sep/Oct), Deepavali (Oct/Nov), Thaipusam (Jan/Feb), Ramadan &
Hari Raya Puasa, Christmas Day, Vesak Day, Good Friday, National Day (9 August).

## Key Practical Tips

1. Carry an umbrella — useful for both sun and the frequent rain; plan indoor activities
   during peak heat or rain.
2. Use the MRT/buses for efficient transport; use Grab for short point-to-point trips.
3. Hawker centres offer the best value and most authentic food.
4. Respect local customs, especially around religious sites and strict laws (drugs,
   e-cigarettes, chewing gum import).
5. English is widely understood; learning a few Singlish phrases is appreciated but not
   necessary.
