# Public Transport in Singapore (Wikipedia)

## Mass Rapid Transit (MRT)

The MRT is Singapore's primary rail backbone. As of November 2022 the network spans 229.7 km
across 166 stations, with six main lines operated by SMRT Trains and SBS Transit. The network
is planned to expand to 360 km by 2030.

## Light Rail Transit (LRT)

LRT systems act as feeder networks to the main MRT in newer towns. Total length is 28.8 km
across three lines in Bukit Panjang, Sengkang and Punggol, using automated rubber-tyred
technology.

## Bus System

Buses are a major part of public transport, with over 4.0 million rides taken per day on
average (2019 figures). More than 365 scheduled services operate through four companies:
SBS Transit, SMRT Buses, Tower Transit Singapore and Go-Ahead Singapore.

## Taxis & Ride-Hailing

Taxis and private-hire vehicles (PHVs, e.g. Grab) are popular alternatives to public
transport. Taxi starting rates are around S$3.20–S$3.90. Taxis can be flagged down outside
the Central Business District; PHVs require app-based booking.

## Fares & Payment

Integrated multi-modal single-journey fares range from about S$0.99 to S$2.26. A Monthly
Travel Pass offering unlimited bus and train rides costs S$128 per month. Fare payment is
integrated across bus and train networks via contactless card (EZ-Link or bank card).

## Private Transport

Private vehicle ownership is very expensive in Singapore due to the Certificate of
Entitlement (COE) system and high taxes — Singapore is considered the most expensive country
in the world to own a car, which is one reason public transport and ride-hailing are the
practical choice for visitors.
